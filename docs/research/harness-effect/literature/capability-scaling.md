# Does scaffolding help weaker models more than stronger ones?

Scope: this note compiles verified literature on whether the benefit of prompting techniques, reflection, tool scaffolds and verification loops shrinks as model capability rises, with per-model deltas where they exist. It serves two purposes, in order: to make a configuration layer around LLM coding agents (instruction files, hooks, skills, subagent routing, stop gates) functionally better, and to support a paper measuring whether such layers change correctness, false completion claims, cost and wall time.

## Sources

1. Wei, J., Wang, X., Schuurmans, D., Bosma, M., Ichter, B., Xia, F., Chi, E., Le, Q., Zhou, D., 2022. Chain-of-thought prompting elicits reasoning in large language models. NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2201.11903. https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903
Claim: chain-of-thought prompting is an emergent ability of scale. Section 3.2: "chain-of-thought prompting does not positively impact performance for small models, and only yields performance gains when used with models of ~100B parameters." Figure 4 shows CoT versus standard prompting across LaMDA, GPT and PaLM scales on GSM8K, SVAMP and MAWPS, with the CoT curve separating from the standard curve only near 100B parameters. Qualitatively, smaller models "produced fluent but illogical chains of thought, leading to lower performance than standard prompting."
Method: few-shot prompting experiments on five model families (LaMDA 422M to 137B, GPT-3 variants, PaLM 8B, 62B, 540B, UL2 20B, Codex) across arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic benchmarks, using a fixed set of eight CoT exemplars.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/html/2201.11903v6. Confidence: high.

2. Yao, S., Zhao, J., Yu, D., Du, N., Shafran, I., Narasimhan, K., Cao, Y., 2022 (revised 2023). ReAct: synergizing reasoning and acting in language models. ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.03629. https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629
Claim: a reasoning-plus-acting scaffold with only one or two in-context examples beats stronger trained baselines. Abstract: "On two interactive decision making benchmarks (ALFWorld and WebShop), ReAct outperforms imitation and reinforcement learning methods by an absolute success rate of 34% and 10% respectively, while being prompted with only one or two in-context examples."
Method: few-shot prompted agents on question answering (HotpotQA), fact verification (Fever) and two interactive decision making benchmarks. Per-benchmark sample sizes are not stated in the abstract.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629. Confidence: high for the quoted abstract claim.

3. Shinn, N., Cassano, F., Berman, E., Gopinath, A., Narasimhan, K., Yao, S., 2023. Reflexion: language agents with verbal reinforcement learning. NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2303.11366. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366
Claim: the benefit of a reflection loop depends strongly on base model strength, and the smallest usable-to-weak model gains nothing. Main text: improvements of +22% on ALFWorld (130 of 134 tasks solved), +20% on HotpotQA, and 91.0 versus 80.1 pass@1 on HumanEval over the GPT-4 baseline. Appendix A, Table 5 (HotpotQA, 100 questions): ReAct baseline plus Reflexion goes 0.30 to 0.55 with text-davinci-003, 0.26 to 0.38 with gpt-3.5-turbo, 0.39 to 0.51 with gpt-4; the relative gain shrinks as the model strengthens. Appendix A, Table 4: with starchat-beta as actor on HumanEval, baseline 0.26 and Reflexion 0.26 averaged over 8 trials, no gain. Appendix A states: "We found that the ability to specify self-corrections is an emergent quality of stronger, larger models."
Method: reflection loops with unit-test self-evaluation on 134 ALFWorld tasks, 100 HotpotQA questions, HumanEval and MBPP; GPT-3, GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and starchat-beta as actors.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366v4. Confidence: high.

4. Chen, B., Shu, C., Shareghi, E., Collier, N., Narasimhan, K., Yao, S., 2023. FireAct: toward language agent fine-tuning. arXiv:2310.05915. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05915
Claim: internalizing a scaffold into weights (fine-tuning on scaffold trajectories) helps weaker models far more, with the relative gain falling monotonically with base capability. Table 2 (prompted ReAct versus fine-tuned, HotpotQA exact match): Llama-2-7B +11.4 points, a 77% relative increase; Llama-2-13B +62%; CodeLlama-7B +60%; CodeLlama-13B +39%; CodeLlama-34B +25%; GPT-3.5 +7.8 points, a 25% increase. Section 5.1: "While weaker LMs benefit more from fine-tuning (e.g., Llama-2-7B increases by 77%), even strong LMs such as GPT-3.5 could improve performances by 25%."
Method: 500 ReAct trajectories generated by GPT-4 used to fine-tune five open models and GPT-3.5, evaluated on 500 HotpotQA dev questions, plus robustness and generalization tests.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05915v1. Confidence: high.

5. Huang, J., Chen, X., Mishra, S., Zheng, H. S., Yu, A. W., Song, X., Zhou, D., 2023 (ICLR 2024). Large language models cannot self-correct reasoning yet. arXiv:2310.01798. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798
Claim: intrinsic self-correction without external feedback degrades performance for every model tested, and hurts the weakest model most. Section 3.2: "after self-correction, the accuracies of all models drop across all benchmarks." Table 3 (GSM8K, standard prompting then two self-correction rounds): GPT-3.5 75.9 to 74.7, GPT-4 95.5 to 89.0. Table 4: GPT-4-Turbo 91.5 to 90.0, Llama-2-70b-chat 62.0 to 36.5. On CommonSenseQA GPT-3.5 falls 75.8 to 41.8. Section 4, Table 7 (GSM8K, equal response budgets): multi-agent debate with 9 responses scores 83.0 versus 88.2 for plain self-consistency with 9 responses. The paper also shows Reflexion-style gains depend on oracle labels that decide when to stop correcting.
Method: GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo and Llama-2-70b-chat on GSM8K (full 1,319-problem test set for GPT-3.5, 200 sampled problems for others), CommonSenseQA and 100 HotpotQA questions, with multiple feedback-prompt variants.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2. Confidence: high.

6. Wang, X., Zhou, D., 2024. Chain-of-thought reasoning without prompting. arXiv:2402.10200. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10200
Claim: CoT paths already exist inside models and are hidden by greedy decoding rather than absent, so part of what prompting scaffolds add is access, not capability. Abstract: "CoT reasoning paths can be elicited from pre-trained LLMs by simply altering the decoding process," and CoT-decoding "effectively elicits reasoning capabilities from language models, which were previously obscured by standard greedy decoding."
Method: top-k decoding-path experiments across standard reasoning benchmarks; exact sample sizes are not stated in the abstract.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10200. Confidence: high for the abstract claim.

7. Snell, C., Lee, J., Xu, K., Kumar, A., 2024. Scaling LLM test-time compute optimally can be more effective than scaling model parameters. arXiv:2408.03314. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314
Claim: the payoff of inference-time scaffolding (verifier-guided search, adaptive revision) depends on prompt difficulty and on base capability; spent optimally, it substitutes for scale. Abstract: "the effectiveness of different approaches to scaling test-time compute critically varies depending on the difficulty of the prompt"; a compute-optimal strategy "can improve the efficiency of test-time compute scaling by more than 4x compared to a best-of-N baseline"; and "on problems where a smaller base model attains somewhat non-trivial success rates, test-time compute can be used to outperform a 14x larger model."
Method: search against process-based verifier reward models and prompt-adaptive distribution updates, FLOPs-matched comparisons on math benchmarks. Sample sizes are not stated in the abstract.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314. Confidence: high for the abstract claims.

8. Brown, B., Juravsky, J., Ehrlich, R., Clark, R., Le, Q. V., Ré, C., Mirhoseini, A., 2024. Large language monkeys: scaling inference compute with repeated sampling. arXiv:2407.21787. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787
Claim: repeated sampling scales coverage massively where verification is automatic, but selection methods (majority voting, reward models) plateau, so the scaffold benefit is capped by verifier quality. Abstract: on SWE-bench Lite "the fraction of issues solved with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct increases from 15.9% with one sample to 56% with 250 samples, outperforming the single-sample state-of-the-art of 43%," while "common methods for picking from a sample collection (majority voting and reward models) plateau beyond several hundred samples."
Method: repeated sampling across four orders of magnitude on coding, math and formal proof tasks, with SWE-bench Lite as the agentic case. Sample sizes per task are not stated in the abstract.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787. Confidence: high for the abstract claims.

9. Xia, C. S., Deng, Y., Dunn, S., Zhang, L., 2024. Agentless: demystifying LLM-based software engineering agents. arXiv:2407.01489. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01489
Claim: a deliberately simple three-phase pipeline (localization, repair, patch validation, no tool loop, no model-chosen actions) beat all open-source agent scaffolds of its time on both performance and cost, evidence that scaffold complexity can be net negative even before model capability is considered. Abstract: "the simplistic Agentless is able to achieve both the highest performance (32.00%, 96 correct fixes) and low cost ($0.70) compared with all existing open-source software agents" on SWE-bench Lite.
Method: fixed three-phase pipeline evaluated on SWE-bench Lite and the harder Lite-S subset, compared against open-source agents.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01489. Confidence: high for the abstract claims.

10. Gao, V., Khosrowshahi, V., Khosrowshahi, A., Sun, X., Lee, J., Lee, S., 2026. Zero-shot self-orchestration with ledger-based control for improved LLM coding performance. arXiv:2608.26480. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26480
Claim: the same manager-worker scaffold has large, null or negative effects depending on model capability and reasoning mode, the most direct scaffold-by-capability interaction measurement found. Abstract, on the 100 latest hard LiveCodeBench problems across nine models (five open-weight from 9B to about 2.8T parameters, four frontier closed models): the scaffold's benefit is "large and statistically significant for some (Qwen3.8-27B +23.4, GPT-5.6-Luna +10.6 and GPT-5.6-Terra +8.0, each over five paired passes; Kimi-K3 +30.4 and Minimax-M3 +11.0 ... both at p < 10^-4)" and "null or negative for others (Qwen3.6-35B -1 to -9 with reasoning off)." With the manager, Opus-5 reaches the study's highest score at 91% in one pass. Running a manager "roughly triples the token bill," yet a mid-tier model with the manager matches a frontier model single-call accuracy (85.0 against 87.4, p = 0.59) at a fifth of the price ($11.71 against $61.11 per 100-problem pass, p < 10^-4). "Improvements are modest for large models with reasoning enabled, but larger for some models with reasoning disabled and for smaller models with reasoning enabled." The recurring mechanisms are context management and problem decomposition.
Method: the same zero-tuning manager-worker scaffold over a shared filesystem, measured against the same model in a single pass, five paired passes per condition on 100 hard LiveCodeBench problems.
Verified by fetching the arXiv API abstract feed for the paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26480 resolves to the same record). Confidence: high for the quoted abstract numbers; medium overall because only the abstract, not the full text with tables, was inspected.

11. Rudyk, A., Oertel, J., Hebig, R., 2026. Aging of prompt engineering techniques across LLM versions. ICSME 2026, arXiv:2608.24641. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24641
Claim: prompt scaffolds age differently per model family, and for the strongest family the gains turn negative across generations. Table VII (successor minus predecessor, average delta in percent relative to zero-shot): GPT family few-shot -7.4, CoT -0.8, CCoT +1.1, PoT +4.2; Mistral family CoT -13.8, PoT -12.1; Qwen family few-shot +7.9, CCoT +11.5. Section V-F: "Newer GPT models exhibit diminishing or even negative marginal gains from structured prompting, suggesting that instruction-following and reasoning scaffolds are increasingly internalized." Pass@k analysis shows GPT-4o gains only +1.13 points from pass@1 to pass@3 while Qwen2.5 gains +5.95, so extra attempts substitute for prompting on strong models.
Method: partial replication of Khojah et al. (2025): five prompting techniques, six instruction-tuned models in three version pairs, 218 context-rich Python functions (195 effective), 19,620 generations scored by pass@k.
Verified by fetching https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24641v1. Confidence: high.

Verified by fetching the page. Confidence: high for what the page claims; the underlying evidence is not published.

13. Yan, W., 2025. Don't build multi-agents. Cognition blog, June 12, 2025. https://cognition.ai/blog/dont-build-multi-agents

## Synthesis

The literature supports a conditional version of the thesis. Across four independent method families, measured scaffold benefit declines as base capability rises. For prompting techniques, Wei et al. (1) place the CoT benefit above roughly 100B parameters, with smaller models sometimes harmed, and Rudyk et al. (11) measure the generational continuation: GPT-family few-shot deltas turned negative (-7.4) while Qwen-family deltas stayed strongly positive (+11.5 CCoT), with the explicit interpretation that reasoning scaffolds are "increasingly internalized." For distilling scaffolds into weights, FireAct (4) shows a clean monotone decline in relative gain, 77% for Llama-2-7B down to 25% for GPT-3.5. For reflection loops, Reflexion (3) shows the per-model gains shrink from +25 points (davinci-003) to +12 (gpt-4) in relative terms on HotpotQA, and states that specifying self-corrections is "an emergent quality of stronger, larger models." For orchestration scaffolds, Gao et al. (10) measure +23.4 for a 27B model against -1 to -9 for a stronger 35B model under the identical scaffold. Snell et al. (7) supply the mechanism-level summary: test-time scaffolding is most valuable exactly when a smaller model has non-trivial success rates, and optimally spent it can beat a 14x larger model.

The evidence also contradicts a naive reading. First, the relationship is not monotone at the bottom: a scaffold can be beyond a model's ability to exploit. Wei et al. (1) found CoT hurts small models, and Reflexion (3, Appendix A) found starchat-beta gained exactly zero from the full reflection loop. The benefit curve is better described as an inverted U: too weak to use the scaffold, no gain; mid strength, maximum gain; strong, gain shrinks toward zero and can go negative. Second, scaffolds that rely on the model judging itself fail at every capability level and fail hardest at the bottom: Huang et al. (5) show intrinsic self-correction degrades GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo and Llama-2-70b alike, with Llama-2 losing 25.5 points on GSM8K while GPT-4 loses 6.5, and shows that published gains vanish once oracle stop signals are removed. Reflexion works where it works because a unit-test suite and a compiler, not the model, decide whether to reflect. Third, verification quality caps the whole family of sampling-and-checking scaffolds: Brown et al. (8) show coverage keeps climbing to 56% with 250 samples while majority voting and reward models plateau, and Reflexion's own false-positive test-execution rate of 16.3% on MBPP (3, Table 2) shows weak verifiers actively producing wrong completions claims. Fourth, minimal is a real baseline: Agentless (9) beat all open-source agent scaffolds of mid 2024 with a three-phase pipeline at $0.70 per instance, and Anthropic (12) reports the same from production deployments. Wang and Zhou (6) suggest why gains compress: much of what reasoning scaffolds add is already latent and merely unlocked.

Open gaps. No published work holds a production-style configuration layer (instruction file plus hooks plus skills plus routing) fixed across more than three model generations and reports per-generation deltas with cost, so the aging result (11) has not been replicated at the agent-configuration level. The interaction between scaffolding and reasoning-native models is only probed at one point (10, reasoning on versus off). Industry positions (12, 13) are consistent with the academic curve but unpublished. Whether instruction-file length behaves like prompt-technique aging, that is, whether long instruction files help weak models and hurt strong ones, is unmeasured anywhere in the verified set.

## What a configuration layer should do differently

1. Key the configuration to model tier and family, and regenerate it on model upgrade. The same instruction file is not a stable artifact: few-shot exemplars, CoT instructions and persona prompts that help one family actively hurt another across a single generation (11: GPT few-shot -7.4, Qwen CCoT +11.5, Mistral CoT -13.8). Mechanism: store instruction files and skill libraries as (model_family, model_generation, technique) tuples, tag each with the last model version it was validated on, and run a regression pass on upgrade; retire any technique whose delta turned negative rather than inheriting it. This follows directly from 11 and from the Reflexion aging pattern in 3.

2. Make heavy scaffolding the small-model profile, not the default. The largest measured wins from exemplars, explicit reasoning instructions, reflection loops and manager-worker orchestration accrue to weak and mid-tier models (4: +77% relative for Llama-2-7B versus +25% for GPT-3.5; 10: +23.4 and +30.4 for small and mid open models; 3: +25 points for the weakest strong model). The frontier-model profile should be close to bare: strong models lose or gain nothing from added prompting (11 GPT deltas near zero or negative; 1: emergence means no benefit below threshold, saturation means no benefit above it), and Anthropic's production rule is to add complexity only when it demonstrably improves outcomes (12). Mechanism: maintain two or three scaffold profiles (bare, standard, heavy) bound to capability tiers, and route automatically: cheap model plus heavy scaffold first, escalate the model rather than the scaffold when quality falls short, because a mid model with a manager matches a frontier model at a fifth of the price (10).

3. Never run a reflection or retry loop without an external stop signal. Intrinsic self-correction degrades every model tested and the weakest most (5: Llama-2 62.0 to 36.5 on GSM8K; GPT-4 95.5 to 89.0), and debate with equal inference budget loses to plain self-consistency (5, Table 7). Reflexion's gains exist precisely where an evaluator outside the model (unit tests, compiler, exact match) gates the loop (3). Mechanism: stop gates may only re-run executable checks (tests, typecheck, build) and may only trigger reflection on a failure signal from those checks; a stop gate that asks the model whether its own work is done without new evidence is contraindicated. Cap retries per signal source, since Reflexion's own false-positive test rate (16.3% on MBPP, 3) means a passing weak verifier must not terminate the loop either; treat a green suite from a thin verifier as necessary, not sufficient.

4. Prefer sampling and cheap verifiers over heavier prompts, but only where automatic verification exists. On verifiable tasks, repeated attempts with selection dominate scaffold elaboration: 15.9% to 56% on SWE-bench Lite with 250 samples (8), and compute-optimal verification-guided spending beats a 14x larger model in its range (7). But selection quality is the binding constraint once attempts are cheap (8, voting plateau). Mechanism: for tasks with executable checks, spend the budget on N runs of the cheap tier plus a deterministic judge (tests), not on richer prompts for the expensive tier; for tasks without executable checks, do not pretend a verifier exists, because reward-model selection plateaus and LLM judges are attackable (5).

5. Constrain subagent delegation to read-only investigation, and keep all writes single-threaded. Parallel workers acting on disjoint context make conflicting implicit decisions and produce integration failures; the fix documented in production is one thread with shared full traces, subagents only for isolated questions whose transcripts are summarized out of the main context (13). The manager-worker gains that do replicate come from context management and decomposition, not from parallel judgment (10). Anthropic's own pattern separates orchestrator-workers for unpredictable subtasks from parallelization for independent subtasks and explicitly prefers simpler patterns (12). Mechanism: allow subagents to search, read and summarize; serialize patch application; require that every writing subagent sees the full trace of decisions that affect its work or does not exist.

6. Put every requirement in the initial instruction; do not budget for discovering constraints through feedback rounds. Huang et al. (5, Section 5) show that apparent self-correction gains often come from requirements stated only in the feedback prompt; moving them into the initial prompt beat the self-correction pipeline outright, and re-adding the correction step lowered performance (81.8 standard versus 75.1 self-corrected on CommonGen-Hard with a strong initial prompt). Mechanism: the instruction file and task templates must carry the complete constraint set up front, hooks should reject prompts that defer requirements to a later review pass, and any improvement claimed by an iteration mechanism must be re-measured against an initial prompt that already states everything (the paper's own fairness rule, applied to configuration).

7. Keep exemplars few and short. The verified prompting results were obtained with one or two in-context examples (2), eight exemplars (1), and two examples per technique (11); nothing in the verified set supports long instruction documents, and the internalization trend (11) predicts their marginal value falls on the strongest models while token cost is unconditional. Mechanism: default instruction files to a small fixed budget of targeted exemplars and measured rules; treat length growth as a cost-bearing change that requires an A/B against the short version, per model tier, before it merges. The Agentless result (9) is the structural analogue: fewer moving parts won on both correctness and cost.

8. Expect scaffolds to expire and budget for their retirement. The edit-apply model is the documented case: a small-model scaffold that was standard practice in 2024 is gone because the large model absorbed the skill (13). Mechanism: every scaffold component (skill, hook, wrapper) carries an expiration test, a small benchmark it must still beat its removal on; components that no longer beat bare-harness behavior on the current model are dropped automatically rather than accumulating.

## Claims to test

1. Scaffold-by-capability interaction on a fixed configuration layer. Run one configuration layer (instruction file plus hooks plus skills) in its heavy and bare variants across at least five models spanning roughly 10B to frontier, on 100 hard coding tasks, five paired runs per condition (design and power follow 10). Outcome: interaction coefficient of scaffold delta versus baseline capability, plus token cost per condition. Prediction from 4, 10, 11: delta positive and largest for mid-tier, near zero or negative for frontier, cost roughly tripled by orchestration.

2. Stop-gate composition versus false completion claims. Three arms on the same 150 tasks: no gate, self-judged reflection gate, executable-test gate. Measure false done rate (agent claims completion, hidden tests fail) and total cost. Prediction from 3, 5, 8: the test gate cuts false completions sharply, the self-judged gate does no better than no gate and costs more, and the gap widens as the base model weakens.

3. Instruction-file length and exemplar count by tier. Four instruction budgets (0, 200, 1k, 4k tokens) crossed with three model tiers on a fixed task set of 100 tasks, three runs each. Outcome: resolve rate and cost per task. Prediction from 1, 11: positive slope for the low tier, flat or negative slope for the top tier, so the optimal length is tier-dependent.

4. Delegation policy under fixed budget. Single-threaded agent versus manager-worker versus parallel writing subagents, 50 multi-file tasks, three runs per arm, equal token budget enforced. Outcome: correctness, wall time, integration conflicts. Prediction from 13, 10: parallel writing loses on correctness at equal budget; manager-worker wins on correctness but at roughly three times the tokens (10).

5. Configuration aging on model upgrade. Freeze the instruction files and skills, bump the model within one family (the 11 design), rerun 195 tasks. Outcome: per-technique delta sign flips. Prediction from 11: at least one technique that helped the predecessor hurts the successor in the GPT-family pattern, which would establish that configuration layers need automated re-validation rather than manual review.

6. Verifier strength as the mediator of reflection gains. Same reflection loop, three verifier strengths (none, weak self-generated tests, full hidden suite) on 100 tasks with one mid-tier model. Outcome: net correctness change over baseline and false positive completion rate. Prediction from 3 (its 16.3% MBPP false positive rate), 5, 8: gains scale with verifier strength, and the weak-verifier arm produces completions that pass the agent's own tests but fail hidden ones.
