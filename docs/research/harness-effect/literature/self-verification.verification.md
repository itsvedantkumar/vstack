## Corrections

1. **Entry 5 (Table 6 range).** Report says: "backtracking improves originally incorrect answers by +18.06 to +43.92 percentage points across 5 tasks." Actual Table 6 gains (oracle location): word sorting +23.53, tracking shuffled objects +43.92, logical deduction +36.86, multistep arithmetic +18.04, Dyck languages +18.06. Corrected statement: "+18.04 to +43.92 percentage points across 5 tasks" (the +18.06 figure belongs to Dyck languages, not the range minimum). All other entry 5 numbers check out.
2. **Body claim attributed to entry 5 (synthesis and "Route on localization" sections).** Report says backtracking is "net-positive once mistake-finding reaches roughly 60 to 70 percent accuracy, a bar prompting a large model does not meet but a small trained classifier can." The paper states the opposite in Section 5.2: "the performance of our classifiers do not meet the threshold required for effective backtracking" (classifier mistake-finding accuracies are 6.0–37.7, far below the ~60–70% the paper identifies). Corrected statement: the small classifier beats prompted large models on 4 of 5 tasks but does not reach the backtracking threshold; the paper leaves closing that gap to future work.
3. **Body claim attributed to entry 1 ("What a configuration layer should do differently").** Report says: "Intrinsic self-judgment loses 3 to 25 accuracy points depending on model and task (entry 1)." Actual losses in Tables 3–6 span roughly 1 to 38 points: GPT-3.5 loses only 1.2 on GSM8K (75.9 → 74.7) but 37.7 on CommonSenseQA after one round (75.8 → 38.1); Llama-2 loses 25.5 on GSM8K (62.0 → 36.5) and 27.5 on CommonSenseQA. Corrected statement: "loses roughly 1 to 38 accuracy points depending on model and task."
