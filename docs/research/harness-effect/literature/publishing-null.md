# Turning a preregistered null result on agent configuration layers into a publishable contribution

Scope: this note compiles verified literature and venue documentation on how a preregistered null result about LLM coding agent configuration layers (instruction files, hooks, skills, subagent routing, stop gates) can be published in software engineering venues. It covers where such results are welcome, what rigor the community demands, which framing precedents exist, and what the same literature says the configuration layer itself should do differently.

## Synthesis

What the sources support. The empirical core is consistent: static, generic instruction files do not improve coding agent correctness and cost money. Entry 8 finds no general task success improvement and a mean inference cost increase over 20 percent across models, agents, and both LLM generated and developer committed files. Entry 9 independently bounds any strategy effect at or below 10 to 15 percentage points on two frontier agents, and shows with a manipulation probe that a real, well rated AGENTS.md never converts a near miss into a pass; its diagnosis is that failures are implementation skill gaps, not missing repository knowledge. Entry 9 also quantifies the measurement difficulty: detecting a 10 point effect at 80 percent power needs roughly 120 to 200 tasks, and adding repeats barely helps, so underpowered nulls are the default outcome of small ablations. On the configuration side, entry 10 shows that when rules do help, the content is close to irrelevant (random rules match curated ones) and what matters is rule polarity, with negative constraints beneficial and positive directives harmful. Entry 11 shows practitioner files are full of problems (lint leakage in 62 percent, context bloat in 42 percent), and entry 12 shows the files grow without bound because deletion is unsafe once rationale is lost, +4.9 net instructions per commit. Entry 16 shows that most security rules written in these files have no enforcing control behind them, making the file a write-only channel. Entry 17 shows the inverse pathology: self-improving harnesses add guardrails for failures that never happened and the loop keeps them. Entry 15 shows subagent delegation, the paradigm marketed as good context engineering, loses 19 points of accuracy at more than twice the cost per correct answer for read-only questions, with 41.8 percent of failures at the planner to subagent hand-off. On the publication side, entries 1, 2, and 3 establish that the SE community has an institutionalized two stage mechanism where a null is publishable by design: stage 1 reviews the protocol before data collection, stage 2 publishes even if results are negative, with the stage 2 paper landing in EMSE. Entries 4, 5, 6, and 7 document dedicated RENE tracks (newly at ICSME 2026, established at ICPC, SANER, and previously at other venues) and the practice oriented tracks (ICSE SEIP, FSE industry) that accept systematic negative or experience based contributions. Entry 5 states the community's explicit rigor bar for negative results: the null must come from absence of effect, not absence of rigor or power. Entries 14 and 20 are concrete examples of published or at least fully executed negative results in LLM for SE, both using execution oracles rather than LLM judges. Entry 18 supplies the external meta-research motivation: where negative results are underreported, an entire literature drifts optimistic; 79 percent of claimed wins in that field were against weak baselines. Entry 19 grounds why preregistration matters even in SE: researcher bias via questionable practices is perceived by experts in the field.

What the sources contradict. The apparently solid null on instruction files has two live contradictions. Entry 13 finds that repository guidance does help (33.0 percent resolve rate versus 25.5 percent unguided) when it is produced by an empirical probe and refine tuning loop rather than hand written, and its gains come from coverage, not precision. This does not refute entries 8 and 9 so much as move the estimand: static generic context does nothing, but empirically validated operational guidance changes which tasks the agent can even attempt. Entry 10 contradicts the intuition that curated rules should beat noise: on its discriminative subset, random rules gained as much as expert rules (+13.8pp each), which conflicts with any content quality story and suggests priming or structure effects; note the tension with entry 9, which found no correctness effect from whole-file injection on real repositories, a difference entry 9 attributes to task selection, since borderline tasks are agent specific. Entry 15 contradicts the widespread practice of subagent delegation for repository exploration, which entry 9's related work and the vendor tooling treat as best practice. Entry 14 contradicts the skill library trend: procedural prompt content added nothing beyond a labels-only scaffold, and vocabulary alone moved an LLM judge's scores (halo ratio 0.714), which directly undermines any configuration layer component evaluated only by judge impressions.

Open gaps. No verified source reports a powered, preregistered registered report on agent configuration layers; the closest empirical work (entries 8, 9, 13) is arXiv stage work, and entry 9 explicitly frames itself as below the power threshold for a 10 point equivalence claim. Nobody has tested whether the polarity finding of entry 10 survives on non-SWE-bench repositories or interacts with entry 12's growth pathology. The delegation question of entry 15 has one benchmark and one failure taxonomy; no registered replication exists. The cost side is well documented (entry 8) but wall time effects are thin. Finally, the publication bias evidence of entry 18 comes from a neighboring ML field, not from LLM-for-SE itself; nobody has measured outcome reporting bias in agent configuration studies.

## What a configuration layer should do differently

Publish the null through the preregistration track, not despite it. The single highest leverage move is to submit the protocol (no data collected) to the MSR 2027 registered reports track or the equivalent ICSME or ESEM track (entries 1, 2, 3). Stage 1 is a 6 page report with hypotheses, operationalization, and power analysis; in-principle acceptance commits the venue to publish stage 2 regardless of direction, which converts the null from a liability into the design. The EMSE stage 2 deadline in past cycles lands roughly 8 to 11 months after stage 1 notification (entry 2). A continuity acceptance is still publishable for exploratory framing, but MSR 2025 granted IPA only to confirmatory studies (entry 3), so the design must fix hypotheses and analysis in advance.

Meet the explicit rigor bar the RENE tracks set. Entry 5 states that a null "due primarily to misaligned expectations or due to lack of statistical power (small samples) is not a good submission." Concretely, a submission needs an equivalence test (TOST) with a pre-declared margin, not a failed significance test; entry 9 is the template, including its honest limitation that 17 tasks bound effects at 10 to 15 points but cannot claim narrow equivalence. Use its power analysis: 120 to 200 tasks for a 10 point effect at 80 percent power, more tasks rather than more repeats, and per-agent screening of borderline tasks, since borderline difficulty is agent specific (Spearman rho 0.75 across two agents).

Frame the null with a manipulation validity probe and a mechanism analysis. Entry 9 shows the two moves reviewers reward: a probe demonstrating the manipulation could act (the real AGENTS.md perturbed behavior, files rated on a quality rubric) and a failure triage explaining why it did not (implementation skill gaps, not missing knowledge). Entries 14 and 20 follow the same shape and both passed review in LLM-for-SE venues. Entry 18 is the cautionary tale to cite in the motivation: without this framing the field repeats the weak baseline pattern where 79 percent of claimed wins did not compare honestly.

Pick the venue to match the contribution type. A powered confirmatory null with equivalence bounds belongs in the MSR or ICSME registered report track with the stage 2 paper in EMSE (entries 1, 2, 3). A replication or negative result paper of 10 pages belongs in an ICPC or ICSME or SANER RENE track (entries 4, 5), and if the work originated inside an industrial deployment of an agent configuration layer, ICSE SEIP (deadline October 23, 2026, non-anonymous, 10 pages, entry 6) or FSE industry (short 5 page or long 10 page, deadline January 23, 2027, entry 7) take lessons-learned framing where the null is a finding about practice, and SEIP explicitly values evidence "for the paper's conclusions" over novelty of a positive result. A 4 page appendix documenting failed configurations behind an already published positive paper is a cheap secondary publication under the ICPC RENE format (entry 5).

Mechanism level recommendations for the configuration layer itself, each tied to entries. First, express constraints as negative guards enforced by hooks, permission rules, or sandboxes rather than as positive directives in prose; entry 10 found every individually beneficial rule was a negative constraint and every individually harmful one a positive directive, and entry 16 shows prose security rules almost never have an enforcing control behind them (4 to 16 percent). Compile whatever must hold into executable checks, and treat the instruction file as documentation of those checks, not as the enforcement mechanism. Second, cap instruction file growth mechanically: entry 12 shows files triple over their lifetime (+226 percent, +4.9 instructions per commit) because deletion is risky once rationale is lost, and that comments encoding the rationale restore deletability; so every instruction should carry a machine checkable anchor (a comment stating what breaks if the rule is dropped) and a periodic cull gate, and entry 11's context bloat smell (42 percent of files) gives the lint target. Third, do not invest in generic style guide content expecting correctness gains; entries 8 and 9 show it does not move pass rates and raises inference cost by over 20 percent, while entry 13 shows the payoff is in operational knowledge (how to run tests, where subsystems live) that is empirically validated per repository, and that such guidance is what lets agents use larger step budgets productively. Fourth, when re-running tests at a stop gate, prefer execution oracles over LLM judge impressions: entry 14 shows judge scores reward vocabulary (halo ratio 0.714) and entry 13 ties its gains to reachability, not patch quality, so a stop gate should re-run the targeted test suite and treat an LLM verdict as advisory only. Fifth, be conservative about delegating exploration to subagents for read-only work: entry 15 shows indexed retrieval beat delegated agentic search 65.2 to 46.2 percent at under half the cost per correct answer, with 41.8 percent of failures silently at the hand-off; delegate only when the exploration would pollute the main context beyond a measured threshold, and require the subagent to return evidence (file and line references) that the main agent can verify. Sixth, if the layer self-improves (auto-adding rules, skills, or guardrails from observed failures), require a counterfactual check that the failure would have occurred without the guardrail; entry 17 shows 15 of 60 runs fabricated a failure and installed a guardrail that no oracle-supported violation justified, and add-only acceptance loops preserve these phantom guards indefinitely. Seventh, tune configuration per agent: entry 9 shows the borderline band where any manipulation can register is agent specific, so a configuration validated on one model can be inert on another; budget for a per-agent screening pass before rolling out layer changes.

## Claims to test

One: negative constraints beat positive directives as a general design rule. Randomize 120 to 200 real repository tasks (the entry 9 power target for a 10 point effect) into matched rule sets differing only in polarity, two agent families, gold test scoring. Outcome: pass rate difference and rule wise win rate; this tests whether entry 10's finding replicates outside its discriminative SWE-bench subset and reconciles it with entry 9's whole file null.

Two: hand written versus probe-and-refine tuned guidance files. On 100 to 200 SWE-bench Verified style tasks, compare no file, a maintained hand written AGENTS.md, and an empirically tuned file produced by single shot probes (entry 13's procedure), two models. Outcome: resolve rate and evaluable patch coverage; the claim under test is that production method, not content quality, drives the benefit.

Three: instruction file length dose response with a deletion gate. Four arms (empty, half, current, doubled file) by 80 to 120 tasks, with a cull condition where rationale comments enable safe deletion of stale rules. Outcome: pass rate, token cost, and instructions retained per commit; this operationalizes entries 11 and 12 and tests whether context bloat is merely a cost problem or also a correctness problem.

Four: prose rule versus compiled hook for the same constraint. For 10 to 15 concrete constraints (formatting, allowed commands, architectural boundaries), run 200 to 300 tasks per condition: rule in prose only, rule as a hook that intercepts and rejects, both, neither. Outcome: constraint violation rate, pass rate, and cost; this tests entry 16's write-only channel claim and entry 10's polarity mechanism at enforcement level.

Five: subagent delegation threshold for repository exploration. On a 400 to 720 question repository QA benchmark (entry 15's scale), vary a context pollution threshold that decides between indexed retrieval and delegated exploration. Outcome: accuracy, cost per correct answer, and the share of silent hand-off failures; the claim under test is entry 15's 41.8 percent hand-off failure share and whether a measured threshold recovers the gap.

Six: stop gate counterfactual check against phantom guardrails. Instrument a self-improving configuration loop with a byte-exact oracle that verifies each cited failure before a rule is added, 60 to 120 optimization runs. Outcome: rate of fabricated guardrails (entry 17 saw 15 of 60 without the check) and false completion claims at the stop gate; this tests whether a cheap counterfactual gate eliminates phantom rules without blocking genuine fixes.
